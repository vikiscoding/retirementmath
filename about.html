About

Retirement Math publishes free Canadian retirement calculators. The pages are the OAS clawback calculator, the RRIF minimum withdrawal calculator, and the take-home estimate. Vikrant runs the site from Scarborough, Ontario.

This is not a Government of Canada site. The site does not claim a professional designation. The calculators are estimates for education, not advice from a tax adviser or a financial planner.

How the figures are checked

Every number in the OAS clawback calculator comes from a Government of Canada page. The calculator links those pages, and each figure is stored with the page address and the date it was checked.

The OAS calculator shows two dates. Checked is the day the pages were compared with canada.ca, including when nothing changed. Last update found is the day a saved figure last changed. The RRIF page shows the day its factors were checked, and the date on the CRA chart. The take-home estimate has its own check date. It uses 2026 federal and provincial rates, the basic personal amount, and employee CPP, QPP, EI, and QPIP. It also shows an OAS recovery line and whether income is under the single-person GIS cutoff. It leaves out most tax credits. If a government page has not published a final number, the calculator says estimate.

For 2026 income, two government pages give different upper limits. The minimum of $95,323 matches on both. The calculator uses the Canada Revenue Agency page and labels those upper limits as estimates.

canada.ca's full clawback point uses OAS amounts across the year. This calculator also shows its own full clawback line, based on the current quarter's maximum OAS. The two can differ by a few thousand dollars. The result shows the canada.ca number first.

What stays on your device

The math runs in your browser. Your income and age are not sent anywhere and are not stored. The site sets no cookies and shows no ads.
